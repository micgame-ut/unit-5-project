# Project Breakdown

> EDITING DIRECTIVE: THE DEVELOPER WRITES THIS FILE AND MAY ASK THE AGENT FOR HELP. THE DEVELOPER MUST REVIEW AND APPROVE ITS CONTENT.

Purpose: Present the finished project, its goals, the decisions behind it, and how it works, as a shareable document. It expands on your showcase stand-up.

## Working agreement

Developer: Write in your own voice. Choose what to show, select and caption the images, and make sure every claim matches the finished project.

Agent: Read the project documents and inspect the project. Help organize, draft diagrams, and tighten wording. Do not invent results, decisions, or evidence.

When you begin this step, start a fresh chat so its transcript stays clean. For example:

`Read the project documents, inspect the finished project, and help me develop my project breakdown.`

Replace the guidance under each heading below with your own content. Save images in this folder and reference them with relative paths. Aim for roughly 4–8 pages, including images.

## Overview

What the project is, who it's for, and why you built it, in one short paragraph.

## Goals

Your experience goals and technical goals, and how they changed after the pitch, if they did.

## Starting point and tech stack

Whether you started from scratch, one of your previous projects, or an open-source project, and the technologies and AI tools you used, with a brief reason for each major choice.

## Design

Where it applies, the art style and how you established and maintained it, the interaction design, and how you kept the visual presentation consistent. Use screenshots, sketches, or reference images.

## How the system works

A plain-language explanation of the architecture: its main parts, how data and state move through it, and how the core interaction loop works. Include at least one diagram. Note how you kept the system lean.

## Process and key decisions

How the project developed from pitch to finished result, and three to five consequential decisions: the alternatives, what you chose, and why. Include scope changes and cuts, and at least one moment where you redirected or corrected an Agent.

## Testing and results

How you verified the project, what peer review revealed, and what you changed in response. Compare the finished project with your goals, including the status of your stretch goals and anything left unfinished.

## Reflection

What you learned, what you would do differently, and where the project could go next.

## Credits and sources

All external code, assets, data, and references, with their sources and licenses.
