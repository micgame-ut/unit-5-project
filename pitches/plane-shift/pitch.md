## Ideation

### Ideas

For each idea worth considering, note what it is, what appeals to you, who it's for, and the main feasibility concerns.

All would be built in Unity for a Windows download. Ideas 4-6 are Agent suggestions, not from the Developer's Notion backlog.

#### 1. Rifter (2D puzzle-platformer) — set aside, became Plane Shift
- **What:** Toska, a 2D being, is trapped in a prison. The player is a 3D being who tears Rifts between dimension layers to free him. Each dimension changes the rules (v1: Prison + Water World).
- **Appeals:** Most developed story and world. Distinct visual hook: each dimension has its own palette, and the Rift previews the next one.
- **Users:** Puzzle-platformer players (Fez, Portal, Lemmings fans).
- **Feasibility:** Rift rendering (mask showing the next layer), per-layer collision rules, and Toska's autonomous movement. Main risk is designing and debugging multi-layer puzzles; Rift rules must read clearly.

#### 2. Wizarding Runes (action combat)
- **What:** Cast spells by drawing runes with a wand. Walking forward vs. backward changes the spell (`>` ice attack, `<` vortex pull), and chosen elemental affinities set the playstyle.
- **Appeals:** Clear spell grammar (rune x movement x affinity). Drawn runes linger as glowing trails, which gives opponents counterplay.
- **Users:** Action and spell-combat players.
- **Feasibility:** Gesture recognition (e.g. the $1 Unistroke algorithm) must be fast and forgiving. Combat adds enemy and arena content. VR vs. flat screen, and PvP vs. PvE, are undecided.

#### 3. Speed Horror (first-person chase)
- **What:** Sprint through an automatically generated forest from safe point to safe point while a pursuer closes in, heard more than seen.
- **Appeals:** Adrenaline-driven chase fantasy; sound-led horror.
- **Users:** Horror players.
- **Feasibility:** Procedural forest that doesn't feel samey, pursuer AI that is scary but fair, 3D positional audio, 3D assets with licensing and polish costs. Objective, pursuer types, and run structure are undecided.

#### 4. Echo (sound-sight puzzler) — Agent suggestion
- **What:** In a dark world, sound pulses reveal geometry for a moment through a shader-drawn wave. Navigate and solve rooms by pulsing.
- **Appeals:** Strong visual identity from a single shader idea; pairs with directional audio.
- **Users:** Puzzle and atmosphere players.
- **Feasibility:** Custom pulse shader and pulse-vs-geometry logic. Level content is the main cost.

#### 5. Ghost Loop (time-echo puzzler) — Agent suggestion
- **What:** Each loop, your previous run replays as a ghost. Cooperate with past selves to solve rooms.
- **Appeals:** One clear mechanic with a deep puzzle space.
- **Users:** Puzzle-platformer players.
- **Feasibility:** Deterministic input recording and replay. Puzzle design and testing are content-heavy.

#### 6. Fold (paper-fold platformer) — Agent suggestion
- **What:** Fold a 2D world along creases to bring distant platforms together.
- **Appeals:** Tactile, readable visual hook; related to Rifter's layer idea.
- **Users:** Puzzle-platformer players.
- **Feasibility:** Mesh deformation and collision after folding is the technical risk; level design is also content-heavy.

#### 7. Plane Shift (working title; 2D puzzle-platformer)
- **What:** A stack of three 2D planes (background, midground, foreground) shares one set of x/y coordinates. The player is in one plane at a time and shifts forward or backward, keeping position and momentum. If a wall blocks the way, find a plane where that spot is open. Each plane has fixed rules: low gravity behind, normal in the middle, heavy in front.
- **Appeals:** Evolved from Rifter's dimension idea into a pure mechanic with a clear core question ("where is there open space in a world I can't fully see?"). Fully specified controls, rules, level structure and color language already exist in the Notion doc.
- **Users:** Puzzle-platformer players (Fez, Portal fans).
- **Feasibility:** Movement and momentum rules per plane, readable layered visuals with parallax, shift-denial logic, carried objects. The main risk is readability across three planes and content volume for 7 teaching steps.

## Pitch

Keep this section to about 1,000 words.

### Concept

What it is, in a few sentences.

Plane Shift is a 2D puzzle-platformer where moving forward or backward feels like entering another dimension. The world is a stack of three 2D planes sharing the same x/y coordinates. You exist in one plane at a time, shift to a neighbor while keeping your momentum, and each plane has its own rules (low gravity behind, normal in the middle, heavy in front). Escape the prison by finding the plane where the wall in front of you is open.

### Title and branding

Your working title, logo, and a short description of the visual branding. These may change during development; keep the current branding with the visual rules in `docs/spec.md`.

Working title: **Plane Shift**. Logo: not yet drawn. Branding: glassy, futuristic, minimalist layered 2D, with translucent planes you can see through; violet background, slate midground, ember foreground; near-white player. See Ideation → Branding.

### Why this project

Why you chose it and what makes it worth five weeks.

Plane Shift grows out of a dimension idea I've carried since 2023 (Rifter). I kept what I cared about, moving between dimensions, and turned it into a mechanic with fixed rules that I can build and test in days. The design is already specified, and the scope is one I can finish in five weeks, so the time goes into making it polished and readable.

### Users

Who it's for and what they need from it.

Puzzle-platformer fans (players of Fez, Portal, Limbo), playing in short sessions on a Windows PC. They need to see where a shift is possible, rules that stay the same across the game, and failure that is cheap. Peer reviewers will play it in one sitting, so the first rooms must teach one new idea at a time.

### Experience goals

What Users should notice, feel, and be able to do.

Users should notice the three distinct planes, with warm in front and cool behind, and always see where a shift is possible. They should feel the difference between floaty, normal and heavy movement, and the satisfaction of finding open space in a world they can't fully see. They should be able to shift planes, shift mid-air to carry momentum across, and solve the opening set of rooms using each plane's rules.

### Technical goals

What the system must do and the technical challenges you intend to take on.

- **Plane stack:** three planes sharing x/y, with only the current plane solid and the neighbors visible but untouchable.
- **Per-plane rules:** fixed gravity and movement values per plane, with momentum adapting on entry (vertical speed scales to the new gravity, horizontal carries over).
- **Shift logic:** one shift per airtime, a blocked-shift rule (camera moves, shakes, returns, player stays), and carried objects that shift with the player.
- **Readability:** parallax per plane, landing ghosts showing where a shift would land, and a player silhouette through foreground solids.
- **Challenge I'm taking on:** making a three-plane stack readable at 60 FPS while keeping the rules simple enough to learn one at a time.

### Starting point

From scratch: a new Unity Universal 2D project. Fez and others are inspiration only. No outside code is used; any adopted code, font or asset will have its license checked first (see `docs/research.md`).

### Tech stack

The technologies you plan to use and why, including any you are new to.

Unity (version to be pinned in `AGENTS.md`), URP with the 2D Renderer, C#, Shader Graph, Unity Input System, Git/GitHub. Windows build as the public delivery (hosting to be decided). None of these are new to me. Chosen because the design is 2D, layered rendering suits Unity's sprite and sorting tools, and I already have a Unity workflow.

### MVP or critical function

The smallest version that genuinely works end to end, or the riskiest function you will validate first, and how you will know it works.

One room with a midground and a background plane: A/D move and jump, W shifts back (S shifts forward once the foreground plane exists, in Phase 3), the plane's gravity changes the jump, and a landing ghost shows where a shift lands. A wall blocks the midground and is open in the background, and an exit door ends the room. It works when a tester who hasn't seen the game can complete the room, a shift into a solid triggers the blocked-shift response, and it runs at 60 FPS.

### Risks

What could block or derail the project, and how you will find out early.

- **Readability across three planes:** players may not see where shifts are possible. *Find out:* test the MVP room with someone new in Phase 2.
- **Parallax and landing ghosts:** values may feel wrong or hurt clarity. *Find out:* tune in the prototype; parallax is already marked as revised in the design doc.
- **Momentum rules:** plane-entry scaling may feel unfair. *Find out:* playtest air shifts in Phase 3.
- **Content volume:** three planes and seven teaching steps need many rooms. *Find out:* count finished rooms at the end of Phase 3 and cut from the end of the teaching order.
- **Plane themes undecided:** background and foreground art could stall. *Find out:* decide by Nov 9.
- **Licensing:** fonts, textures, audio. *Find out:* check each before adoption and log in `docs/research.md`.
